**Software Engineering & Architecture**

IBM Think 2026 delivered what the company calls a "Blueprint for the AI Operating Model" — a framework where AI agents are embedded into every layer of the enterprise stack, from IT ops to developer pipelines. IBM's data shows a widening "AI divide" between companies that have restructured around agents and those still treating AI as a productivity bolt-on, and the gap increasingly maps to engineering culture: teams with platform abstractions that expose clean APIs to agents are accelerating; those without are stalling.

**Enterprise AI & AI Strategy**

A report released Monday finds 46% of enterprise AI initiatives have failed to meet expectations despite 74% of organizations increasing investment — with only 29% reporting significant ROI from generative AI. The gap between "frontier" companies (deploying 3.5x more AI per employee) and laggards is structural: most organizations are still buying access rather than embedding AI into operational workflows. Separately, Capgemini today announced an investment in OpenAI's Deployment Company, joining 18 other global firms in the new majority-OpenAI-owned enterprise deployment vehicle.

**AI Tools & Developer Utilities**

Anthropic's Code with Claude conference (SF last week, London up next on May 19) shipped three features that reframe what agent infrastructure means: Multiagent Orchestration for parallelizing work across agent fleets, Outcomes for defining measurable success criteria so agents self-correct, and Dreaming — a between-session process where agents review their own prior work and write new memory entries for future sessions. The telling signal: Anthropic released no new models, explicitly framing the bottleneck for production agent systems as infrastructure, not model capability.

[Code with Claude 2026: 5 New Agent Features Anthropic Just Shipped](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/code-with-claude-2026-new-agent-features)
