**Software Engineering & Architecture**

Google I/O (May 18–20) introduced WebMCP, a proposed open web standard that lets developers expose structured tools so browser-based AI agents can execute tasks directly—an experimental origin trial starts in Chrome 149. Alongside it, Antigravity 2.0 shipped as an agent-first dev platform with built-in subagent orchestration, cross-platform terminal sandboxing, and hardened Git policies baked in. The arc is clear: the IDE is becoming a multi-agent runtime, and the browser isn't far behind.

**Global Macro & Markets**

Progress toward a US-Iran agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz sent Brent crude down more than 5% to ~$98/barrel on Friday—its worst weekly performance in months. Trump said the deal is "largely negotiated" but sticking points remain over Iran's enriched uranium stockpile and Hormuz transit tolls. With ~20% of global oil and LNG flowing through the strait (traffic nearly halted since February strikes), a resolution would significantly ease supply anxiety and give central banks more room on inflation. US equity markets are closed today for Memorial Day.

**Enterprise AI & AI Strategy**

A new Writer survey finds 79% of enterprises face challenges adopting AI—a double-digit jump from 2025—even as 59% spend over $1M annually on it. Only 29% report significant ROI from generative AI, and just 23% from agents. Gartner is predicting 60% of agentic AI projects will collapse this year due to data readiness failures, not model quality. The dominant pattern emerging: companies have AI strategy documents that no one uses (75% of execs admit theirs is "more for show") while the firms actually extracting value have quietly embedded AI into core operational workflows with shared data standards.

**AI Tools & Developer Utilities**

Anthropic's May updates to Claude Managed Agents added multiagent orchestration—a lead agent can now break jobs into pieces and delegate to specialists with their own models, prompts, and tools running in parallel on a shared filesystem. Also shipped: MCP tunnels for routing agents through private networks without public exposure, and a "dreaming" research preview that lets agents review past sessions to find patterns and self-improve. Meanwhile, Claude Code gained an agent view for managing multiple background sessions from a single CLI, a direct response to the volume problem that emerges when you're running dozens of parallel coding agents.
