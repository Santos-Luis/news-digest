📅 Daily Digest — June 12, 2026

**Software Engineering & Architecture**

Sonar held a live demo on June 11 showcasing its acquisition of Gitar, an AI-native code review platform that embeds automated review directly into the development lifecycle. The company also published results from its SonarSweep service—a data-cleaning layer for LLM training sets—showing that models trained on swept data produce up to 67% fewer security vulnerabilities and 42% fewer bugs than those trained on raw code, quantifying the compounding quality problem at the root of AI-generated code defects. [Sonar Acquires Gitar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260521la64832/sonar-acquires-gitar-expanding-code-verification-platform-to-include-ai-code-review)

**Global Macro & Markets**

May PPI came in sharply above consensus on June 11—headline up 1.1% vs. 0.7% expected, with annual wholesale inflation running at 6.5%, the hottest reading since late 2022. Futures markets now price a ~70% probability of at least one Fed hike this year, with October emerging as the most likely meeting; new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh faces the data head-on at next week's FOMC, where updated economic and rate projections are expected to reflect a materially more restrictive path. **Enterprise AI & AI Strategy**

JPMorgan Chase formally reclassified its AI investments from experimental R&D to core infrastructure, backing the move with a $19.8 billion technology budget and a dedicated team of 2,000 staff. The shift matters less for the dollar amount than for the accounting signal: treating AI as infrastructure rather than a bet changes capital allocation, depreciation schedules, and board-level accountability—and with Gartner projecting 40% of enterprise applications will include task-specific AI agents by year-end, JPMorgan is likely not alone. [OpenAI: The Next Phase of Enterprise AI](https://openai.com/index/next-phase-of-enterprise-ai/)
